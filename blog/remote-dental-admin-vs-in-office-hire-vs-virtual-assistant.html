Blog · October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Most dental practices end up trying all three of these at some point. Here is how they compare on the things that matter: cost, dental knowledge, time to fill, and whether the person is still there in a year.

In-office hire

Best when: the role needs to be physically present (greeting patients, handling the operatory schedule in real time).

The catch: the local pool for experienced dental admins keeps shrinking, seats sit open for months, and once you add payroll tax, benefits, PTO, recruiting, and equipment the real cost is roughly double the hourly rate. Burnout and turnover in the admin seat are the biggest hidden expense most practices carry.

Generic virtual assistant

Best when: the work is truly general (inbox cleanup, data entry, social media scheduling).

The catch: insurance verification, claims, and credentialing are not general. A VA who doesn't know dental has to be taught what a frequency limitation is, how to read an EOB, and why a claim came back needing a narrative. You end up as the trainer, and when they leave for the next gig you do it again. Many VA arrangements also skip HIPAA training and never sign a BAA, which is a compliance problem waiting to happen.

Dedicated remote dental admin team member

Best when: you need someone who already knows the work, will work only for your practice, and will still be there next year.

How Denterra does it: we recruit people abroad who already have insurance verification, billing, or related admin experience, verify their HIPAA knowledge, dental terminology, top procedure codes, practice management software, and carrier portal skills, then present pre-screened candidates for you to interview. You choose. We handle pay, equipment, secure workstation setup, performance management, and quarterly KPI reviews. The team member joins your practice full-time at a flat $15 an hour.

Because team members earn 2 to 3 times local pay and have a real career path, they stay. That is the piece a generic VA or a revolving front desk seat never gives you: the ability to invest in someone and have the investment compound.

Which should you pick?

If the seat is patient-facing, hire locally. If the work is non-dental and low stakes, a generic VA is fine. If the work is insurance verification, claims and collections, treatment coordinator support, patient coordination, or credentialing, a dedicated remote dental admin is the option that holds up over time. FAQ

Related questions.

Is a remote dental admin the same as a virtual assistant?
Not really. A generic VA does general admin work for multiple clients. A Denterra remote dental admin has prior dental or medical admin experience, verified dental skills, HIPAA training, and works full-time for one practice.
Can a remote team member work inside my practice management software?
Yes. Denterra team members are introduced to Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental and work inside your system through secure workstation tooling with role-based access.
What roles can a remote dental admin fill?
Insurance verification, claims and collections, treatment coordinator support, patient coordinator (recall, confirmations, payment plans), credentialing, and for larger groups an admin team lead.
